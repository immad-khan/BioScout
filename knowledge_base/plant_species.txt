The flora of the Margalla Hills is diverse, changing with altitude and exposure. Dominated by subtropical pine forests, the area also features deciduous trees and scrubland. Common tree species include Chir Pine, Acacia modesta, and Olea ferruginea. The undergrowth is rich with various shrubs, grasses, and medicinal plants. Many wildflowers bloom seasonally, adding to the park's beauty. Protecting these plant species is vital for the entire ecosystem, providing food and shelter for wildlife.
# Flora of Margalla Hills National Park (MHNP)

The Margalla Hills, forming the foothills of the Himalayas, are home to over **600 plant species**, contributing significantly to Pakistan’s biodiversity.

## Key Plant Species

- **Chir Pine (Pinus roxburghii)**: The dominant conifer species found above 900 meters elevation.
- **Phulai (Acacia modesta)**: A drought-resistant tree important for soil retention and bee foraging.
- **Kahu (Olea ferruginea)**: Also called wild olive, provides habitat and food for various bird species.
- **Sanatha (Dodonaea viscosa)**: A hardy shrub with erosion-control properties.
- **Grewia villosa** and **Justicia adhatoda**: Medicinal herbs commonly found in lower altitudes.

## Seasonal Vegetation

- Spring (March–May): Wildflowers like **anemones**, **irises**, and **buttercups** bloom.
- Monsoon (July–August): Lush growth of grasses and ferns.
- Winter (Nov–Feb): Deciduous trees shed leaves, revealing tree bark textures and bird nests.

## Conservation Challenges

Illegal grazing, invasive species like *Lantana camara*, and urban encroachment threaten native flora. Efforts by IWMB and WWF-Pakistan include habitat restoration and community awareness programs.
